## Executive summary

Non-medical grooming tools are a plausible Midnight Store test category because they are compact, non-fragile, reusable, easy to demonstrate in short social videos, and do not require medical claims. The strongest initial opportunities are a **self-cleaning slicker brush**, a **silicone grooming/bathing glove**, and a **dual-sided deshedding and detangling comb**. Each has clear consumer demonstrations: pressing a button to eject collected fur, pulling loose hair from a glove, or showing the before/after removal of undercoat and tangles.

Demand is supported by a large, active international category and visible marketplace competition. Walmart’s slicker-brush page shows more than 1,000 results and labels several low-priced brushes “Best seller,” including a self-cleaning model with 830 reviews and a small cat slicker with 591 reviews.[1] Alibaba search pages show thousands of pet-grooming-glove listings and low minimum orders, while supplier pages show self-cleaning brushes at roughly $0.32–$2.90 per unit depending on design and MOQ.[2] [3] These are **directional signals rather than verified Lesotho demand or delivered quotations**.

The recommended launch sequence is to test the self-cleaning slicker brush first, add the silicone glove as a low-cost impulse/bundle item, and test the dual-sided comb only after checking teeth quality and snagging performance. A practical first assortment is two or three neutral colors, unbranded packaging, and a short demonstration video focused on loose fur and ease of cleaning. Avoid electric/vacuum grooming devices at launch because they add battery, electrical, noise, warranty, and shipping complexity.

## Evidence and interpretation

### Trend and category signal

Public market-research pages indicate continued growth in pet grooming products, although the freely accessible Grand View Research page does not expose the headline forecast figures in its extracted text and the full report is paid.[5] A separate search result for a pet-grooming-glove market report states a 2025 value of $487.3 million and projected 2034 value of $792.6 million at a 5.8% CAGR.[6] That figure should be treated as **directional and not independently verified from the report body**. The more reliable conclusion is qualitative: grooming tools are an established, recurring-use pet-supplies category rather than a novelty dependent on one season.

### Marketplace demand and competition

Walmart’s live category page reports “Slicker Brush (1000+)” and exposes multiple best-seller markers. The Vibrant Life self-cleaning slicker brush is shown at $9.24 with 830 reviews, the small cat slicker at $4.82 with 591 reviews, and the slicker-plus-cleaning-comb at $8.83 with 174 reviews.[1] Review counts and best-seller labels are marketplace-specific and do not establish sales in Lesotho, but they are useful evidence that the product type has sustained consumer activity in a large retail market.

The same Walmart page shows a broad price ladder: approximately $4.82–$9.24 for simple mass-market brushes, $12.99 for a two-piece slicker/comb listing, and $22.08–$22.99 for more premium or breed-focused products.[1] This supports a Midnight Store retail test around $8–$18 for generic tools, with room for a bundle or premium packaging if the delivered cost allows it.

The same supplier page lists an OEM/ODM double-sided pet hair-removal brush at $1.04–$1.18 with MOQ 100.[3] This supports supplier availability for the third candidate, but the MOQ is less suitable for an unvalidated first order than the very low-MOQ slicker listings.

## Recommended test plan

Start with **one self-cleaning slicker brush SKU** and one **glove pair SKU**. Order samples from at least two suppliers for each product. Test the brush on short and medium coats, checking whether pins are too sharp, whether the ejector button jams, and whether the handle survives repeated use. Test the glove for hand fit, left/right-hand usability, silicone odor, shedding pickup, and cleaning time.

Use unbranded or Midnight Store-branded packaging only after confirming that the supplier is not using copied logos, character artwork, or protected brand names. A small pilot could use 20–50 units per winning SKU if freight economics permit. Bundle a brush and glove at a modest discount to raise average order value and make the demonstration more compelling.

For content, show real fur removal without promising treatment of skin conditions, parasites, allergies, pain, circulation problems, or other medical outcomes. Use captions such as “helps remove loose fur” and “routine grooming tool.” Do not use breed-specific or veterinary endorsements unless substantiated and authorized.

## Risks

1. **Lesotho demand is unverified.** International marketplace reviews, search results, and supplier availability are proxies. Run a small paid-social or organic pre-order test and measure messages, clicks, deposits, and completed purchases before committing to volume.
2. **Landed cost may be materially higher than unit cost.** Small shipments to Lesotho can have disproportionate freight, brokerage, duty, VAT, and last-mile costs. Quote a delivered shipment, not just an Alibaba product price.
3. **Commodity competition is intense.** The large Alibaba catalog and Walmart’s low prices indicate limited product defensibility. Compete through local stock, clear demonstrations, bundle pricing, packaging, and responsive service.
4. **Quality and pet-safety issues are possible.** Bent metal pins, sharp edges, brittle plastic, glove seams, or strong silicone odor can lead to complaints or minor injury. Sample-test every supplier and avoid aggressive “de-shedding” claims.
5. **Sizing and coat suitability vary.** A glove may work better on short coats than dense or matted coats. A slicker brush can snag or irritate if used with excessive pressure. Include simple usage instructions and advise stopping if the pet shows discomfort.
6. **Cross-border returns are uneconomic.** Keep the assortment simple, inspect incoming stock, and set a clear local exchange policy for unused items. Avoid electrical, battery-powered, vacuum, heated, or steam products in the initial test.
7. **Platform signals can mislead.** TikTok’s $35 glove listing has 0 sold, and marketplace review counts are not comparable across sites. Treat all demand and price signals as directional until validated with Lesotho customer behavior.
